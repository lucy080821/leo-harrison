# Word stress

## Syllables and stress

A **syllable** usually has a vowel as its core: *banana* has 3 syllables (ba-na-na). In a word of several syllables, **one syllable is more prominent**, called the **stressed** syllable.

A stressed syllable is typically:

- **louder**, **longer**, **higher in pitch**
- with a **clear vowel**, while unstressed syllables often reduce to `/ə/`.

In transcription, `/ˈ/` goes before the syllable with **primary stress** and `/ˌ/` marks **secondary stress**: *photography* `/fəˈtɒɡrəfi/`.

## Why Vietnamese speakers should care

Vietnamese is a **tonal** language: pitch separates *ma, mà, má*. English uses pitch for stress and intonation, while the meaning of a word depends on **where the stress is**. Stressing the wrong syllable can make a word harder to recognise than mispronouncing one consonant.

## Rules that help you predict

These are **tendencies**, not absolute laws.

| Case | Stress | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Two-syllable nouns and adjectives | usually the **first** | *TAble, HAPpy* |
| Two-syllable verbs | usually the **second** | *deCIDE, reLAX* |
| Endings *-tion, -sion, -ic, -ity* | the syllable **just before** the ending | *eduCAtion, ecoNOmic, possiBIlity*... |
| Endings *-ee, -eer, -ese* | **the ending itself** | *employEE, engiNEER, JapaNESE* |

## Noun and verb pairs

Some words are **spelled the same** but shift stress by word class:

| Word | Noun | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| record | `/ˈrekɔːd/` | `/rɪˈkɔːd/` |
| present | `/ˈprezənt/` | `/prɪˈzent/` |
| import | `/ˈɪmpɔːt/` | `/ɪmˈpɔːt/` |

## Compounds

For **compound nouns**, stress usually falls on the **first part**: *BLACKbird* (a kind of bird) differs from *black BIRD* (a bird that is black).

## A habit

When you learn a new word, **learn its stress together with its sounds**. Open the dictionary and look for the `/ˈ/` mark.
