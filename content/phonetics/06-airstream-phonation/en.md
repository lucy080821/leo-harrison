# Airstream mechanisms and phonation types

## Airstream mechanisms

Speech sounds need an obstructed or compressed airflow. Three parts can **initiate** the airstream:

| Mechanism | Initiator | Sounds produced |
|---|---|---|
| **Pulmonic** | lungs and diaphragm | most sounds; **egressive** (outgoing) is the commonest |
| **Glottalic** | glottis raised or lowered | **ejectives**, **implosives** |
| **Velaric / lingual** | tongue body and velum | **clicks** |

- **Pulmonic egressive**: used by every language. English uses only this. Vietnamese uses mainly this; only the two sounds spelled *b* and *đ* are usually described as implosives or preglottalised stops (module 7).
- **Ejectives** (e.g. `[p'] [t'] [k']`): the glottis closes tightly and rises, compressing the air in front of the closure, which is then released. Because the glottis is closed, ejectives are **voiceless**. They occur in many languages such as Quechua and Amharic.
- **Implosives** (e.g. `[ɓ] [ɗ]`): the glottis **lowers** during the closure, usually with voicing, so they are **voiced**. The airflow does not actually have to go inward.
- **Clicks** (e.g. `[ǀ] [ǃ] [ǁ]`): two closures are made in the mouth and the tongue body is lowered to rarefy the air between them. They are ordinary sounds in languages of southern Africa. English has the "tsk tsk" sound `[ǀ]` but only outside ordinary vocabulary.
- The proportions of languages with each type (ejectives about 16%, implosives about 13%, clicks under 2%) are statistical estimates from surveys, so treat them as approximate.

## Phonation types

**Phonation** is how the vocal folds (and arytenoids) behave. The main types:

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Voiceless** | no vibration, arytenoids apart | `/s/`, `/p/` |
| **Modal voice** | regular vibration with full contact | ordinary vowels |
| **Breathy voice** | vibration without clear contact, much air | some "breathy" vowels |
| **Creaky voice** (laryngealisation) | slow, irregular vibration, little air | utterance ends for some speakers |
| **Whisper** | narrowed glottis creating friction, no vibration | whispering |
| **Falsetto** | stretched, thin folds | high voice |

In some languages (such as Vietnamese) **phonation type is part of the tone**: the ngã and nặng tones often come with creaky voice or glottal closure.

## Voicing and VOT

Whether a stop is voiced or voiceless also depends on **when** the vocal folds start vibrating relative to the release of the closure. That is **VOT** (*voice onset time*), covered in detail in module 3.
