# The vowel chart

Consonants have clear places (lips, teeth, ridge...). Vowels are "softer": air flows **freely** through the mouth and only the **shape of the mouth** changes. So we describe vowels with three criteria.

## Three criteria

1. **Tongue height** (*height*): tongue raised (mouth nearly closed, **close**) or lowered (mouth open, **open**).
2. **Frontness** (*backness*): the highest part of the tongue is towards the front (**front**), middle (**central**) or back (**back**).
3. **Lip rounding** (*rounding*): lips **rounded** or **spread**.

Try saying *see* and then *saw* slowly: you feel the jaw drop and the lips round.

## The 12 monophthongs of British English

| | Front | Central | Back |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Close** | `/iː/` *see*, `/ɪ/` *sit* | | `/ʊ/` *put*, `/uː/` *food* |
| **Mid** | `/e/` *bed* | `/ɜː/` *bird*, `/ə/` *about*, `/ʌ/` *cup* | `/ɔː/` *saw* |
| **Open** | `/æ/` *cat* | | `/ɑː/` *car*, `/ɒ/` *hot* |

This chart is **simplified** to be easy to read. In reality the exact position of a few sounds (for example `/ʌ/`) varies a little between speakers.

## Rounded or not

- **Rounded**: `/ʊ/ /uː/ /ɔː/ /ɒ/`.
- The other vowels have **spread or relaxed** lips.

## Compared with Vietnamese (for reference)

A few English vowels are close to Vietnamese ones, which gives you a starting point:

| English vowel | Close to Vietnamese |
|---|---|
| `/iː/` | a long "i" |
| `/e/` | "e" or "ê" |
| `/ʊ/ /uː/` | short / long "u" |
| `/ɔː/` | a long, rounded "o" |
| `/ɜː/ /ə/` | "ơ" |

**Close does not mean identical**: this is only a starting point. The real difficulty of English vowels is **length** and **quality**, which we cover next.
