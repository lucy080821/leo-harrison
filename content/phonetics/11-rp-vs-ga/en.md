# Where British and American differ

Good news: the two accents differ **in regular ways**, so you don't have to memorise word by word. This lesson summarises the biggest differences between **RP** (British) and **GA** (American).

## Some example words

| Word | RP (UK) | GA (US) |
|---|---|---|
| car | `/kɑː/` | `/kɑːr/` |
| bath | `/bɑːθ/` | `/bæθ/` |
| hot | `/hɒt/` | `/hɑːt/` |
| go | `/ɡəʊ/` | `/ɡoʊ/` |
| better | `/ˈbetə/` | `/ˈbeɾɚ/` |
| dance | `/dɑːns/` | `/dæns/` |
| new | `/njuː/` | `/nuː/` |
| tomato | `/təˈmɑːtəʊ/` | `/təˈmeɪɾoʊ/` |

## Five main differences

**1. /r/ after a vowel.** GA is *rhotic*: *car, hard, teacher* all pronounce /r/. RP drops it, unless a vowel follows (*car engine* still links the /r/).

**2. The vowel in bath, dance, can't.** RP uses a long `/ɑː/`, GA uses `/æ/` (known as the *TRAP-BATH split*).

**3. The vowel in hot, stop.** RP has a rounded `/ɒ/`, GA usually has an open, unrounded `/ɑː/`.

**4. The diphthong in go.** RP `/əʊ/`, GA `/oʊ/`.

**5. /t/ between vowels.** GA turns it into a **flap** `/ɾ/` (a very quick tap of the tongue): *water, better, city* sound close to having a /d/. RP keeps `/t/`.

There is also *yod-dropping*: *new, tune* are `/njuː/` (RP) and `/nuː/` (GA).

## Vocabulary and spelling

Some words differ in sound and also in meaning or spelling (*schedule*, *lift* and *elevator*, *colour* and *color*). That lies outside a phonetics course.

## So which one to choose?

- **Both are standard.** Neither is "more correct".
- Choose **one as your "home accent"** for speaking and try to be **consistent**: don't mix /ɡəʊ/ and /ɡoʊ/ in the same sentence.
- But **understand both**, because films, news and friends will bring you both.
