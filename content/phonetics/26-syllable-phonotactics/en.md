# The syllable and phonotactics

## Syllable structure

A **syllable** consists of:

- **Onset**: the consonants before the vowel.
- **Nucleus**: usually a vowel.
- **Coda**: the consonants after the vowel.
- **Rhyme**: nucleus + coda.

For *strengths* `/streŋθs/`: onset `/str/`, nucleus `/e/`, coda `/ŋθs/` (many speakers say `/ŋkθs/`).

## The maximum structure

English syllables can be summarised as **(C)³V(C)⁴**: at most **3** consonants at the start and **4** at the end.

- Complex **onsets**: *split, spring, street, scream* (s + stop + approximant).
- Complex **codas**: *texts* `/teksts/`, *sixths* `/sɪksθs/`, *strengths*.

Compared with Vietnamese (module 7): Vietnamese has **no** consonant clusters and only one final consonant. This is a significant source of difficulty for learners.

## Sonority

**Sonority** is how "vowel-like" a sound is. A commonly used scale:

**stop < fricative < nasal < lateral < approximant < vowel**

(the further right, the more sonorous). The **sonority sequencing principle**: sonority **rises** from the start of the syllable to the nucleus and then **falls** towards the end. For example *plan* `/plæn/`: /p/ (stop) → /l/ (lateral) → /æ/ → /n/ (nasal).

There are big exceptions: **s + stop** clusters such as *spy*, *stay* violate the principle (fricative then stop), which is why /s/ is often regarded as **special** at the start of a syllable.

## Phonotactic constraints

Each language **allows** some sound sequences and **forbids** others. Some English constraints:

- `/ŋ/` does **not** begin a word.
- `/h/` does **not** end a syllable.
- `/ʒ/` is very rare word-initially (mostly loanwords such as *genre*).
- A three-consonant onset always begins with `/s/`.
- No `/pw/`, `/tl/`, `/sr/` word-initially.

Phonotactics explains why *blick* **could** be an English word but *bnick* **could not**: a nonce word that obeys the constraints.

## Syllabic consonants

Highly sonorous consonants (`/n/`, `/l/`, sometimes `/m/` and `/r/`) can be the **nucleus** of a syllable without a vowel: *button* `[ˈbʌtn̩]`, *bottle* `[ˈbɒtɫ̩]`, *rhythm* `[ˈrɪðm̩]`.

## Syllable boundaries

Dividing syllables after a short stressed vowel with a single consonant (as in *city*, *butter*) is **unclear**: the middle consonant may belong to both syllables (*ambisyllabic*). This is directly related to **flapping** in GA.
