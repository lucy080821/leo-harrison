# The speech organs

To understand sounds, it helps to know where they are made. Your body already has a very refined "instrument". Let's go from the bottom up.

## The journey of a sound

1. The **lungs** push a stream of air upward. Most English sounds use *outgoing* air.
2. The **larynx** holds the **vocal folds**. They can vibrate or stay still.
3. The air passes through the **oral cavity** and sometimes the **nasal cavity**, where the lips, tongue and jaw shape it into different sounds.

## Voiced and voiceless

Put a finger on your throat and hold "sssss", then "zzzzz".

- With **zzzzz** you feel a buzz: this is a **voiced** sound.
- With **sssss** there is none: this is a **voiceless** sound.

This single detail separates many word pairs: *bag* and *back*, *zoo* and *Sue*.

## The way to the nose: the soft palate

At the back of the roof of your mouth is a soft part called the **velum** (soft palate). When it is **raised**, air goes through the mouth only. When it is **lowered**, air also escapes through the nose and we get nasal sounds like `/m/`, `/n/`, `/ŋ/`. Try pinching your nose and saying "mama": it feels odd because the air is blocked.

## Parts of the mouth

| Part | Name | Example sounds |
|---|---|---|
| Both lips | bilabial | `/p/ /b/ /m/ /w/` |
| Upper teeth and lower lip | labiodental | `/f/ /v/` |
| Teeth | dental | `/θ/ /ð/` |
| The bump behind the teeth | alveolar ridge | `/t/ /d/ /s/ /z/ /n/ /l/` |
| Hard palate | palatal | `/j/` |
| Soft palate | velar | `/k/ /ɡ/ /ŋ/` |
| Gap between the vocal folds | glottis | `/h/` |

## The moving part and the fixed part

To make a sound, one part **moves** (the *active articulator*), usually the tongue or lower lip, towards a **fixed** part (the *passive articulator*) such as the ridge or the palate. For `/t/`: the tongue tip (moving) touches the alveolar ridge (fixed).

You don't need to memorise the table yet. In later lessons, each time a new sound appears we will come back to exactly the part we need.
