# Consonants: place, manner and voicing

## Three criteria for describing consonants

1. **Voiced / voiceless** (*voicing*): do the vocal folds vibrate?
2. **Place of articulation**: which moving part approaches which fixed part.
3. **Manner of articulation**: the degree and kind of obstruction of the airflow.

Further options: **central / lateral** (air over the middle or the sides of the tongue), **nasal / oral** (velum lowered or raised), **airstream** (see the previous lesson).

## Place of articulation

| Place | Main parts | English examples |
|---|---|---|
| Bilabial | lower lip + upper lip | `/p b m w/` |
| Labiodental | lower lip + upper teeth | `/f v/` |
| Dental | tongue tip + teeth | `/θ ð/` |
| Alveolar | tip or blade + alveolar ridge | `/t d s z n l/` |
| Postalveolar | blade + back of the ridge | `/ʃ ʒ tʃ dʒ ɹ/` |
| Retroflex | tongue tip curled back | none (Hindi, Tamil) |
| Palatal | tongue body + hard palate | `/j/` |
| Velar | tongue body + soft palate | `/k ɡ ŋ/` |
| Uvular | tongue body + uvula | none (French `[ʁ]`) |
| Pharyngeal | tongue root + pharyngeal wall | none (Arabic) |
| Glottal | the two vocal folds | `/h/`, `[ʔ]` |

## Manner of articulation

| Manner | Description |
|---|---|
| **Plosive (stop)** | complete closure then release |
| **Nasal** | closure in the mouth, air through the nose |
| **Trill** | the moving part vibrates in the airflow |
| **Tap, flap** | one very quick contact |
| **Fricative** | a narrow gap creating friction |
| **Lateral fricative** | fricative with air over the sides of the tongue |
| **Approximant** | very light obstruction, no friction |
| **Lateral approximant** | approximant with air over the sides of the tongue |
| **Affricate** | a plosive followed by a fricative (symbols `tʃ`, `dʒ`) |

## Reading the name of a sound

The standard way: **voiced/voiceless + place + manner**.

| Symbol | Name |
|---|---|
| `/b/` | voiced bilabial plosive |
| `/s/` | voiceless alveolar fricative |
| `/ŋ/` | (voiced) velar nasal |
| `/ɹ/` | voiced alveolar (or postalveolar) approximant |
| `/tʃ/` | voiceless postalveolar affricate |

## Where are English consonants?

English uses 24 consonants: `/p b t d k ɡ tʃ dʒ f v θ ð s z ʃ ʒ h m n ŋ l ɹ j w/`. English has **no** retroflex, uvular or pharyngeal sounds and uses only a few cells of the chart. That explains why learners of English struggle with foreign sounds from other languages and vice versa.
