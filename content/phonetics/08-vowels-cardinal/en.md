# Vowels and the cardinal vowels

## Why are vowels harder to describe?

For vowels the air flows **freely** through the mouth; there is no obstruction to point to. Only the **shape of the mouth** changes. Moreover, the tongue is constantly moving, so its exact position is hard to feel. Phoneticians therefore use a **reference system**.

## Three articulatory criteria

1. **Height**: the tongue raised (**close**), lowered (**open**), or in between (**close-mid**, **open-mid**).
2. **Backness**: the highest point of the tongue is **front**, **central** or **back**.
3. **Rounding**: lips **rounded** or **unrounded**.

Add **length** and **nasalisation** where relevant.

## The cardinal vowels

The British phonetician **Daniel Jones** proposed the **cardinal vowel** system (definitions 1 to 8 appeared in his pronouncing dictionary of 1917). These are fixed **reference points**, not the vowels of any particular language. Phoneticians are trained to recognise and produce these points, and then describe real vowels by their distance from them.


![Cardinal vowels](/phonetics/cardinal-vowels.svg)
*Figure 2.2. The eight primary cardinal vowels (1 to 8) of Daniel Jones.*

| No. | Symbol | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `[i]` | close, front, unrounded (tongue as high and as front as possible without friction) |
| 2 | `[e]` | close-mid, front, unrounded |
| 3 | `[ɛ]` | open-mid, front, unrounded |
| 4 | `[a]` | open, front, unrounded (tongue front and lowest) |
| 5 | `[ɑ]` | open, back, unrounded |
| 6 | `[ɔ]` | open-mid, back, rounded |
| 7 | `[o]` | close-mid, back, rounded |
| 8 | `[u]` | close, back, rounded (tongue as high and as back as possible, lips rounded) |

**Secondary cardinal vowels 9 to 16** have the same tongue positions as 1 to 8 but **reverse the rounding**: for example `[y]` is the rounded counterpart of `[i]`, and `[ɯ]` is the unrounded counterpart of `[u]`.

## Where are English vowels?

English vowels (standard British) are `/iː ɪ e æ ɑː ɒ ɔː ʊ uː ʌ ɜː ə/`. Their positions on the IPA chart **vary by author and accent**, and are a topic for module 5. In module 3 you will see the same vowels on the **formant chart** and the correspondence between the two descriptions.

## A note on accuracy

Cardinal vowels are a **learned system**: they can be learned from guided recordings and practice, not derived from verbal description alone. Modern research often uses acoustic measurements (formants) to describe vowels, instead of or alongside this reference system.
