# The sound systems of RP and GA

From this module we enter **English phonetics**, with the two most described standard accents: **RP** (*Received Pronunciation*, also called *General British*) and **GA** (*General American*).

## Consonants

Most accents have **24 consonants**:

`/p b t d k ɡ tʃ dʒ f v θ ð s z ʃ ʒ h m n ŋ l ɹ j w/`

There is also `/x/` with limited distribution (for example *loch* in some accents). The main consonant difference between the two accents is **/ɹ/**: GA is **rhotic** (pronounces /ɹ/ after vowels), RP is **not**.

## Vowels

The number of vowels **depends on the analysis**:

- **RP**: usually counted as **20** vowels (12 monophthongs and 8 diphthongs following Roach and Gimson), up to **25** depending on the author.
- **GA**: about **14 to 16** vowels.

The numbers differ because authors differ on which sequences count as one diphthong and which as a vowel plus /r/.

## Wells' lexical sets

To compare vowels across accents without assigning symbols in advance, **John Wells** (1982) proposed **lexical sets**: each keyword stands for **a whole class of words** sharing a vowel in a reference accent. For example KIT is the class *kit, bit, sit...*

## Notable mergers

- **LOT-PALM**: in GA, *hot* and *palm* share the vowel `/ɑ/`. RP distinguishes `/ɒ/` and `/ɑː/`.
- **THOUGHT-LOT (cot-caught)**: in many parts of the United States, *cot* and *caught* sound alike.
- **NORTH-FORCE**: *horse* and *hoarse* are alike in both RP and GA.
- **Wine-whine**: in GA generally these two words are alike (no `/ʍ/`).

## Why lexical sets?

An IPA symbol like `/æ/` can stand for **different vowel qualities** in different accents. Keywords let us talk about the **word class** (TRAP, BATH) first, assign symbols afterwards, and compare accents fairly.
