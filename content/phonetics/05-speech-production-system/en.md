# The speech production system

Speech is produced by three coordinated subsystems: **respiratory**, **laryngeal** (phonation) and **supralaryngeal** (articulation).

## 1. The respiratory system: the energy source

The lungs and respiratory muscles push air upward. When speaking we inhale quickly and exhale **slowly and evenly** according to the needs of the utterance. For most sounds in the world's languages, the **initiator** of the airstream is the lungs.

## 2. The larynx: phonation

The **larynx** is a cartilage structure on top of the trachea, made of the thyroid, cricoid and two arytenoid cartilages. The two **vocal folds** stretch between the thyroid and the arytenoids; the **glottis** is the gap between them.

- Glottis **open**: air passes freely, a **voiceless** sound.
- Glottis **narrowed** with air passing: the folds **vibrate**, a **voiced** sound.
- The vibration rate is the **fundamental frequency F0**, which we perceive as **pitch**.

## 3. The supralaryngeal tract

Above the larynx lie the **pharynx**, the **oral cavity** and the **nasal cavity**. We change its shape with the articulators. The distance from the glottis to the lips in an adult (male) is about 17 cm, so it behaves like a resonating tube (see module 3).

## Moving parts and fixed parts

| **Moving** (active) | **Fixed** (passive) |
|---|---|
| lower lip | upper lip, upper teeth |
| **tongue**: tip, body, root | alveolar ridge, hard palate, soft palate, uvula, pharyngeal wall |
| vocal folds, arytenoids | |

The tongue is divided into the **tip**, **blade**, **body (dorsum)** and **root**. Consonants are described by a pair (active articulator, passive articulator), for example `/t/`: tongue tip or blade and the alveolar ridge.

## The velum and nasal sounds

The **velum** (soft palate) can **rise** to block the way to the nose (oral sounds) or **lower** to open the velopharyngeal port (nasal sounds such as `/m n ŋ/`). Vowels can also be nasalised when the velum lowers partially, as in French *bon* `[bɔ̃]`.

## A small experiment

Say *sssss* then *zzzzz* with a hand on your throat. The only difference between the two is whether the vocal folds vibrate (`/z/`) or not (`/s/`). The tongue position near the ridge is identical.
