# Final consonants and clusters

## Why do final sounds go "missing"?

Vietnamese has only **six main final consonants**: `p, t, k, m, n, ng`. English has many: *dog, bus, milk, help, desks, strengths*. Because the mouth is used to stopping early, many learners drop final sounds: *class* sounds like *cla*, *like* like *lai*.

Final sounds matter because they **often carry grammar**: plural (*cat* → *cats*), past tense (*walk* → *walked*), third person (*work* → *works*).

## Two common mistakes

1. **Dropping the final sound**: *help* said as *hel*.
2. **Adding a vowel** after the final sound: *milk* said as "mi-luh-k". Stop at the consonant; don't add the "uh".

## Consonant clusters

English allows **several consonants in a row**, especially at the end of a word: *texts* `/teksts/` has four in a row. Tip: **say each sound slowly first, then speed up**, without inserting vowels between them.

## The -s / -es ending: three ways

The pronunciation depends on the **last sound of the base word**:

| Last sound of the base | Pronounced | Example |
|---|---|---|
| voiceless (`/p t k f θ/`) | `/s/` | cats `/kæts/` |
| voiced and vowels | `/z/` | bags `/bæɡz/`, days `/deɪz/` |
| `/s z ʃ ʒ tʃ dʒ/` | `/ɪz/` | boxes `/ˈbɒksɪz/` |

## The -ed ending: also three ways

| Last sound of the base | Pronounced | Example |
|---|---|---|
| voiceless (`/p k f s ʃ tʃ/`) | `/t/` | worked `/wɜːkt/` |
| voiced and vowels | `/d/` | played `/pleɪd/` |
| `/t d/` | `/ɪd/` | wanted `/ˈwɒntɪd/` |

The general rule is neat: **the following sound "copies" the voicing of the one before it**. Voiced follows voiced, voiceless follows voiceless. Phonetics calls this *voicing assimilation*.
