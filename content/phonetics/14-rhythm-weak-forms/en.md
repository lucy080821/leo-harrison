# Rhythm, weak forms and schwa

## Two kinds of rhythm

Languages have different "beats":

- **English** is usually described as *stress-timed*: **stressed syllables** come at fairly regular intervals, and unstressed ones are **squeezed** to fit.
- **Vietnamese** is usually described as *syllable-timed*: each syllable has a similar length and each one is clear.

So when English is spoken with a Vietnamese beat, it sounds "even and stiff", while native speakers hear more "rhythm" when you squeeze the weak sounds.

## Content words and function words

| Type | Includes | Usually |
|---|---|---|
| **Content words** | nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs | **stressed** |
| **Function words** | articles, prepositions, pronouns, auxiliary verbs, conjunctions | **weak** |

For example: *I WANT to GO to the SHOP.* Only the three capitalised words are stressed.

## Weak forms

Function words have a **strong form** (when stressed or alone) and a **weak form** (in normal speech):

| Word | Weak form | Strong form |
|---|---|---|
| the | `/ðə/` | `/ðiː/` |
| a | `/ə/` | `/eɪ/` |
| to | `/tə/` | `/tuː/` |
| for | `/fə/` | `/fɔː/` |
| and | `/ən/`, `/n/` | `/ænd/` |
| of | `/əv/` | `/ɒv/` |
| can | `/kən/` | `/kæn/` |
| was | `/wəz/` | `/wɒz/` |

*a cup of tea* in normal speech is `/ə ˈkʌp əv ˈtiː/`. But at the end of a sentence or when emphasised, the strong form is used: *"Yes, I can."* `/kæn/`.

## Schwa /ə/: the most common vowel

`/ə/` is a **short, relaxed sound in the middle of the mouth**, and it is the **most frequent** vowel in English. It only appears in **unstressed syllables**:

- *about* `/əˈbaʊt/`
- *banana* `/bəˈnɑːnə/`
- *teacher* `/ˈtiːtʃə/`
- *today* `/təˈdeɪ/`

Spelling varies (a, e, o, u...) but the sound is the same `/ə/`.

## Practice tips

1. Read a sentence aloud and **clap** on the content words.
2. Say the function words **quickly and lightly** between the beats.
3. Don't be afraid to "swallow" words: that is natural English.
