# Reading IPA and the two accents

## What is IPA?

**IPA** (the *International Phonetic Alphabet*) is a set of symbols where **each symbol stands for one sound**. That lets us write down pronunciation without being fooled by spelling. Every good dictionary shows IPA.

## Two kinds of brackets

| Brackets | Name | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `/ ... /` | phonemic transcription | writing phonemes, dictionary style. Example `/kæt/` |
| `[ ... ]` | phonetic transcription | writing the detailed real sound. Example `[kʰæt]` |

This course mostly uses `/ /`. We discuss `[ ]` in module 5.

## "Tricky" symbols

Some symbols look like letters but are read differently:

| Symbol | Sounds like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `/j/` | "y" in *yes* | *yes* `/jes/` |
| `/ʃ/` | "sh" | *ship* `/ʃɪp/` |
| `/ŋ/` | the "ng" at the end of *sing* | *sing* `/sɪŋ/` |
| `/θ/` | unvoiced "th" | *think* `/θɪŋk/` |
| `/ð/` | voiced "th" | *this* `/ðɪs/` |
| `/ɡ/` | "g" in *go* | *go* `/ɡəʊ/` |
| `/ə/` | a very light "uh" | *about* `/əˈbaʊt/` |

## Common marks

- `/ˈ/` **primary stress**, placed just *before* the stressed syllable: *about* `/əˈbaʊt/`.
- `/ˌ/` **secondary stress**: *education* `/ˌedʒuˈkeɪʃn/`.
- `/ː/` **long sound**: *see* `/siː/`.

## Two main accents: RP and GA

- **RP** (*Received Pronunciation*): the traditional standard British accent, common in British dictionaries such as Cambridge and Oxford.
- **GA** (*General American*): the general American accent, the base of most American film and media.

Both are standard, neither is "more correct". Dictionaries usually give both: UK then US. For example:

| Word | RP (UK) | GA (US) |
|---|---|---|
| car | `/kɑː/` | `/kɑːr/` |
| go | `/ɡəʊ/` | `/ɡoʊ/` |
| hot | `/hɒt/` | `/hɑːt/` |

The course will point out where the two accents differ. Pick one as your "home accent" and **stay consistent**; that is enough. Lesson 11 goes deeper.
