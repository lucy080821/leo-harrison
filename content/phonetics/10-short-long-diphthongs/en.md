# Short, long and diphthongs

## Easily confused short and long pairs

Vietnamese does not use **length** to separate meaning as much as English does. So these pairs are often heard as the same:

| Short | Long | Word pair |
|---|---|---|
| `/ɪ/` | `/iː/` | *ship* `/ʃɪp/` and *sheep* `/ʃiːp/`, *sit* and *seat* |
| `/ʊ/` | `/uː/` | *full* `/fʊl/` and *fool* `/fuːl/`, *pull* and *pool* |
| `/e/` | `/æ/` | *bed* `/bed/` and *bad* `/bæd/` |
| `/ʌ/` | `/ɑː/` | *cut* `/kʌt/` and *cart* `/kɑːt/` |
| `/ɒ/` | `/ɔː/` | *cot* `/kɒt/` and *caught* `/kɔːt/` |

It is not only length; the **quality** also differs: `/ɪ/` is a bit more relaxed and lower than `/iː/`. When practising, aim for both.

## Length also changes with the next sound

A vowel is **longer** before a voiced consonant: *bead* `/biːd/` has a longer vowel than *beat* `/biːt/`. Native speakers use this length to tell whether the final sound is voiced or voiceless, so don't ignore it.

## Diphthongs

A **diphthong** is a vowel that **glides** from one position to another within one syllable. British English has 8:

| Diphthong | Example | Glides towards |
|---|---|---|
| `/eɪ/` | *face* `/feɪs/` | `/ɪ/` |
| `/aɪ/` | *price* `/praɪs/` | `/ɪ/` |
| `/ɔɪ/` | *choice* `/tʃɔɪs/` | `/ɪ/` |
| `/əʊ/` | *go* `/ɡəʊ/` | `/ʊ/` |
| `/aʊ/` | *mouth* `/maʊθ/` | `/ʊ/` |
| `/ɪə/` | *near* `/nɪə/` | `/ə/` |
| `/eə/` | *square* `/skweə/` | `/ə/` |
| `/ʊə/` | *cure* `/kjʊə/` | `/ə/` |

Note: many British speakers now say `/ʊə/` as `/ɔː/` (*sure* `/ʃɔː/`), and American English has a slightly different set of diphthongs. We compare them in the next lesson.

## Tips for Vietnamese speakers

- Keep **short vowels really short**; don't lengthen everything.
- With diphthongs, **don't split them into two separate sounds**: say *price* as one gliding sound `/aɪ/`, not "a-i".
