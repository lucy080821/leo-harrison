# Consonants that are hard for Vietnamese speakers

First, some good news: **you don't need perfect pronunciation** to be understood. This lesson only points out the sounds that *often cause trouble*, so you know where to pay attention.

Many sounds feel hard because **Vietnamese does not have them**, so the mouth is not used to them yet. That is completely normal.

## 1. /θ/ and /ð/ ("th")

Vietnamese lacks these two sounds. Learners often replace them with `/t/`, `/s/` (for `/θ/`) and `/d/`, `/z/` (for `/ð/`).

- How: **place the tongue tip lightly between or behind the front teeth** and let air flow.
- `/θ/` has **no throat buzz**: *think, three, bath*.
- `/ð/` has a **buzz**: *this, that, mother*.

> Tip: the Vietnamese letters "th" (as in "thôi") stand for an **aspirated** sound, not `/θ/`. That is why the spelling can mislead you.

## 2. /ʃ/ and /ʒ/

`/ʃ/` ("sh") as in *ship, wash*. The lips are slightly rounded and the tongue is a little further back than for `/s/`. Many people say `/s/` instead, so *she* sounds like *see*. `/ʒ/` is rarer, as in *vision* `/ˈvɪʒn/`.

## 3. /tʃ/ and /dʒ/

`/tʃ/` as in *chair*, close to Vietnamese "ch". `/dʒ/` as in *jump* is **voiced**. It is often pronounced as `/z/` or `/j/`.

## 4. /r/ and /l/

- `/l/`: the tongue tip **touches the ridge** (*light, feel*).
- English `/r/`: the tongue tip **curls slightly up without touching**, with slightly rounded lips (*right, red*).

Minimal pairs to practise: *light* and *right*, *lock* and *rock*.

## 5. /v/ and /w/

- `/v/`: **upper teeth on the lower lip**, with a throat buzz (*very, vest*).
- `/w/`: **both lips rounded** like a "u" (*west, wet*).

Pairs to practise: *vest* and *west*, *vine* and *wine*.

## Gentle practice

1. Pick **one** pair of sounds per week.
2. Say the minimal pairs slowly and watch your mouth in a mirror.
3. Record yourself and listen back. No scoring, just notice the difference.
