# Teaching pronunciation

This lesson is for teachers (and self-learners who want to coach themselves). It draws on well-known research on pronunciation teaching.

## The goal: intelligibility and confidence

For most learners a sensible goal is **to be understood easily** and **to feel confident speaking**, not to erase the accent. A clear Vietnamese-accented English with correct stress is entirely a good English accent.

## Step 1: Diagnose

1. Have the learner **read a short passage** and **speak freely** for a few minutes, and record it.
2. Listen back and note errors in four groups: **individual sounds**, **word stress**, **rhythm and weak forms**, **intonation**.
3. Compare with the predictions from Vietnamese (lesson 19) to understand **why**.

## Step 2: Set priorities with "functional load"

You can't fix everything at once. One way to choose is **functional load**: how many **word pairs** does a contrast distinguish in English?

- **High**: `/p/`-`/b/`, `/l/`-`/r/`, short-long vowels such as `/ɪ/`-`/iː/`. Confusing them easily misleads the listener.
- **Low**: `/θ/`-`/ð/` (almost no useful minimal pairs). It can wait.

## Step 3: A five-stage teaching framework

Celce-Murcia, Brinton and Goodwin (1996) suggest:

1. **Description and analysis**: show learners how the sound is heard and made.
2. **Listening discrimination**: listen and choose the right sound.
3. **Controlled practice**: read prepared word pairs and sentences.
4. **Guided practice**: prompted activities such as gap fills and short dialogues.
5. **Communicative practice**: use the sound in free speech.

## Useful techniques

| Technique | Use for |
|---|---|
| **Minimal pairs** | listening and speaking confusable sounds |
| **Shadowing** (speaking right after a recording) | rhythm, stress, intonation |
| **Backchaining** | long words and sentences: *-ity, -bility, possibility* |
| **Gestures and visuals** | stress (clapping, a rubber band), tongue position (mirror, diagrams) |
| **Recording and listening back** | self-monitoring and seeing progress |

## Giving feedback

- Be **selective**: one or two points at a time.
- **Praise first**, correct second, and give the learner a chance to try again.
- Avoid interrupting while the learner is speaking freely. Note it and give feedback afterwards.

## A 15-minute activity

1. Hear the teacher say *ship* and *sheep*; learners raise a hand on the long sound. (discrimination)
2. The teacher shows the different mouth shapes. (description)
3. Repeat the word pair, then the sentence *The sheep is on the ship.* (controlled)
4. Learners make up a sentence with both words. (guided)
5. Pairs ask and answer freely about a trip to the sea. (communicative)
