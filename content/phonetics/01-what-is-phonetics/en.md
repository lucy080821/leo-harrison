# What is phonetics?

Hello! This course is about **the sounds of English**. You don't need any background. Each lesson takes about 8 minutes to read, with a few small questions at the end, and **mistakes are fine**. Just try again.

## Sounds and letters are different things

Vietnamese spelling is quite "honest": what you see is mostly what you say. English is not. Look at five words that all contain "ough":

| Spelling | Transcription (British) |
|---|---|
| though | `/ðəʊ/` |
| through | `/θruː/` |
| tough | `/tʌf/` |
| cough | `/kɒf/` |
| bough | `/baʊ/` |

The reverse also happens: one sound can be spelled many ways. The sound `/iː/` appears in *see, sea, key, field, machine*. So to speak well we learn by **sound**, not by guessing from **spelling**. That is the job of phonetics.

## What does phonetics study?

**Phonetics** is the study of speech sounds. It has three main branches:

- **Articulatory phonetics**: how sounds are made with the lips, tongue, larynx and so on. This course follows this approach.
- **Acoustic phonetics**: looking at sound as waves, measuring frequency and intensity.
- **Auditory phonetics**: how the ear and brain perceive sounds.

Closely related is **phonology**: how sounds *work inside a language*, for example why `/p/` and `/b/` separate *pin* from *bin*. We come back to it in the last module.

## A few terms to get comfortable with

| Term | Short meaning |
|---|---|
| Sound (*phone*) | A specific sound that we produce |
| Phoneme | The smallest unit that can change the meaning of a word |
| Transcription | Writing sounds down with IPA symbols |
| Segmental | About individual sounds |
| Suprasegmental | About stress, rhythm and intonation |

## A note before you start

The goal is not to sound like a native speaker overnight. The goal is to **understand what you are doing when you speak**, so that you can correct yourself and be understood more easily. Small steps are enough.
