# Phonemes and allophones

By now you are used to `/ /`. This lesson goes deeper into the question: **when are two sounds counted as "the same sound"?** It is the foundation of phonology and very useful for teachers.

## Phoneme

A **phoneme** is a sound unit that **changes the meaning of a word**. The test is to find a **minimal pair**: two words that differ in exactly one sound.

*pin* `/pɪn/` and *bin* `/bɪn/` differ in meaning only because of `/p/` versus `/b/`. So `/p/` and `/b/` are **two different phonemes** in English.

## Allophone

One phoneme can be pronounced **slightly differently** depending on its position, **without changing meaning**. Each such pronunciation is an **allophone**. We write them in `[ ]`.

### Example 1: aspiration

Hold a thin sheet of paper in front of your mouth and say *pin* then *spin*. With *pin* the paper flutters because `/p/` is **aspirated** `[pʰ]`. With *spin* the paper hardly moves because `/p/` is **not aspirated** `[p]`.

| Word | Phoneme | Actual sound |
|---|---|---|
| pin | `/p/` | `[pʰ]` aspirated |
| spin | `/p/` | `[p]` unaspirated |

Native speakers treat the two as **the same sound** and do not notice the difference.

### Example 2: "l" before and after

| Word | Sound |
|---|---|
| *leaf* (before a vowel) | `[l]` "clear l" |
| *full* (after a vowel, word-final) | `[ɫ]` "dark l", back of the tongue pulled back |

### Example 3: the flap and the glottal stop

- American English: *butter* `[ˈbʌɾɚ]` with `/t/` as a **flap** `[ɾ]`.
- Many British speakers in casual speech: *button* `[ˈbʌʔn̩]` with `/t/` as a **glottal stop** `[ʔ]`.

## Link with Vietnamese

Vietnamese treats **t** `/t/` and **th** `[tʰ]` (aspirated) as **two different phonemes**: *tai* differs from *thai*. English treats `[t]` and `[tʰ]` as just **allophones** of `/t/`. The fact that each language "cuts" sounds in its own way is an important source of difficulty in learning pronunciation.

## Broad and narrow transcription

- **Broad** (`/ /`): records phonemes, simple, used in dictionaries. Example `/pɪn/`.
- **Narrow** (`[ ]`): records the detailed real pronunciation. Example `[pʰɪn]`.

For teaching pronunciation, broad transcription is usually enough. Narrow transcription is useful when you analyse a specific accent.
